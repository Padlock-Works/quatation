Questions to ask on your visit

Current situation
How do you currently take and track reservations — phone calls, WhatsApp, paper register, or something else?
Do you ever lose bookings or have double-booking problems? How often?
How do guests find you? (walk-in, referral, Booking.com, social media)
Do you have a website right now? If yes, can you show me?
Technology comfort
Do your staff use smartphones or a computer daily for the business?
Do you have reliable electricity and internet at the property?
Has anyone here used a booking or hotel software before?
Hosting & budget
Would you prefer software that runs only on your own computer, or one you can access from anywhere via the internet?
Are you open to a small monthly fee for hosting and maintenance, or do you prefer a one-time payment?
What is your rough budget for something like this — just so I can tailor what I recommend?
Features & priorities
What is your single biggest pain point in managing bookings today?
Do you want guests to be able to book and pay online themselves, or just staff managing reservations internally?
Do you accept M-Pesa? Would you want that integrated so payments appear automatically in the system?
Do you work with agents or OTAs (Booking.com, Airbnb)? Would you need the system to connect with them?

Marketing & sales tips

Lead with the pain, not the technology
Don't open with "I build Django systems." Open with: "Do you ever lose a booking because it was written on paper and misplaced?" Make them feel the problem before you offer the solution.
Build one demo they can touch on your phone
A live working demo on your mobile browser is worth 100 slides. Show them: add a booking, check room availability, print a receipt. If they can touch it, they believe it.
Offer a starter tier to reduce friction
Small guesthouses fear big commitments. Offer a "basic local system" at a low once-off price to get your foot in the door. Upsell hosting, website, and online bookings later once they trust you.
Target clusters, not individuals
Visit a street or area with many lodges in one day. When one says yes and their neighbor sees it working, the neighbor calls you. Referrals in tight hospitality communities are powerful.
Speak their language: "reduce lost revenue"
One double-booking or missed reservation can cost a lodge KES 3,000–10,000. Frame your system as preventing that loss. "My system pays for itself in 2 months" is a powerful closer.
Use WhatsApp as your sales channel
After a visit, follow up on WhatsApp with a short video of your demo. Lodge owners are more likely to watch a 60-second screen recording than read a PDF proposal.
Offer a free 2-week trial (local install)
Install the local version on their PC at no charge for 2 weeks. After two weeks of using it daily, very few will want to go back to a notebook. Then convert to a paid plan with hosting.
Collect a testimonial from your first client
A short quote and photo from even one happy lodge owner becomes your most powerful sales material. Show it to every subsequent prospect on your phone.
